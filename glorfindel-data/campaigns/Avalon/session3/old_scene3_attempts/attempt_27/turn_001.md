In the waning hours of twilight, the reeds swayed as the breath of Avalon whispered through them, carrying with it an inescapable sense of the unnatural. The coracle, a vessel half-embraced by the water's edge, awaited your approach, its occupants watching you intently.

You, Sioned ferch Cai, strode forth with purpose, the parchment clutched tightly in your hands. Upon reaching the waiting figures, you presented them with the artifact that had brought you here, the very evidence of their debt now laid before them like a deed to a long-lost inheritance.

A silence descended upon the scene as the three figures exchanged glances. One spoke, a voice deep and resonant, "You come bearing a debt, maiden. We have names: Gwyn ap Nudd, Lord of Annwn; Rhiannon, the queen of this enchanted realm; and Owain, the bard who sings our tales. You seek to close an account, but what price are you willing to pay for your freedom?"

You stood firm, meeting their gaze with unwavering resolve, "I am willing to pay any price that allows me to leave before dawn."

The three exchanged glances once more, then Gwyn ap Nudd spoke again, his voice solemn, "We demand an offering, a token from your world. Something of value to you, but also something that will serve as a reminder of this encounter. If we deem it sufficient, you may depart."

You considered their terms for a moment, weighing the cost against the time you had left. In that instant, a sense of urgency surged within you, and you knew what you must do to secure your freedom. With a swift motion, you reached into your cloak and withdrew an ornate brooch—a gift from your father, Caius ap Llywarch, emblazoned with the crest of the Lion Rampant. Offering it to Gwyn ap Nudd, you said, "This is all I have that holds any value for me."

The three figures conferred once more, their eyes never leaving the brooch in your hand. Time seemed to slow as they debated your offering's worthiness. At last, Rhiannon spoke, her voice soft yet authoritative, "Your token is accepted, Sioned ferch Cai. You have until dawn to depart."

As you turned to leave, a cold breeze swept across the water, and you felt a presence behind you—the thing that had been following you in the reeds. You knew it was not yet time to face whatever lurked there, but as the sun began its descent towards the horizon, the shadows grew long, and the true test of your courage lay ahead.
