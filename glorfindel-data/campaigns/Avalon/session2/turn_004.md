In the fading light of twilight, Peredur found himself standing before an ancient shrine, its once grand facade now crumbling and overgrown with ivy. The door, a heavy oak adorned with runes, seemed to loom ominously against the backdrop of a setting sun. With a deep breath, he raised his candle high, casting long shadows that danced upon the ancient stone.

The moment his knuckles made contact with the cold wood, a chill ran through him. His heart pounded in his chest as if trying to escape. The door creaked open slowly, revealing an abyss of darkness within. A faint glow emanated from deeper within, drawing Peredur in like a moth to a flame.

Stepping into the shadows, he found himself in a chamber unlike any other he had ever encountered. The air was thick with an otherworldly energy that hummed softly in his ears. To his left stood a figure shrouded in darkness, its form barely discernible against the wall. As Peredur approached, it stepped forward, revealing itself to be the Fisher King.

His eyes met Peredur's with a piercing gaze, filled with an emptiness that seemed to reach deep into his soul. In that moment, Peredur felt a wave of dread wash over him. His mind raced as he searched for the words he had come to say, but they seemed lost in the darkness around them.

With a trembling voice, Peredur spoke the question he had written so many nights ago: "Who will heal thee, and how shall I know the time?" The words hung heavy in the air, echoing through the chamber. The Fisher King regarded him for what felt like an eternity before responding in a voice that seemed to resonate throughout the world:

"Thou shalt be healed when thou findest me one who is wounded yet not bleeding. In a time when winter's icy grip has loosened its hold and spring's first blossoms bloom anew."

As Peredur turned to leave, the Fisher King spoke again, his voice echoing softly behind him: "And remember, child of Percival, the wound is not always visible."
