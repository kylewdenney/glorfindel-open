# Fae Rituals — Laws of the Otherworld

## The Absolute Laws

These are not rules enforced by authority. They are the grammar of how the Otherworld functions. Violating them doesn't cause punishment — it causes consequences that the Fae find predictable and the violator finds catastrophic.

**True Names bind.** A Fae who learns your true name may use it once to compel a single honest answer or a single action. You may never lie to someone who holds your name. Giving your name freely is an offering of significant weight.

**Hospitality is law.** Food and shelter accepted creates a temporary truce. Harming a host or guest while hospitality holds breaks something in the Fae who witnesses it — they will act against the violator, not from anger but from necessity.

**Bargains hold.** A bargain made in the presence of a Fae — even casually, even metaphorically — is binding. "I'd give anything to find him" is an offer. The Fae are precise. They heard you.

**Beauty and danger are the same thing.** The most beautiful Fae are not necessarily the most powerful, but they are the most dangerous to spend time with. Something that looks like grace is often an opening.

**Time is not consistent near the Veil.** Hours inside a Fae place may be days outside, or years, or moments. The Fae do not consider this a problem.

## Opening Contact

To initiate a ritual with intent:
- You need a **threshold** — a doorway, a body of water, a standing stone, a fire lit with old wood
- You need an **opening phrase** — this can be in any language, but the Fae respond better to Gaelic, Welsh, or Latin spoken by someone who knows what the words mean
- You make the **RITUAL check** — DC 14 for a stable threshold, DC 18 for open ground

On failure: nothing answers. On a natural 1: something answers that you did not invite.

## The Shape of a Bargain

Fae bargains have three parts, always:
1. **What is given** (the Offering — what you put forward)
2. **What is received** (the blessing, the knowledge, the passage)
3. **When it is settled** (the condition under which the debt closes)

Part three is where mortals make mistakes. "When the quest is complete" sounds finite. The Fae heard "when the quest is complete to my satisfaction." They know things about quests that you don't.

## Types of Fae Encountered

**The Sídhe** — the old ones, the people under the hills. They remember the original knights. They have been waiting with the patience of things that do not die. They are not cruel but they are not kind. They are *interested*.

**The Fair Folk** — smaller, stranger, attached to specific places. A river has its folk. A crossroads has its own. They are more capricious and less patient than the Sídhe. Their bargains are smaller and more literal.

**The Liminal** — creatures that exist at thresholds: dusk, dawn, shorelines, the moment before sleep. They do not bargain but they carry messages, sometimes without knowing it. Reading a Liminal is a VEIL check.

**Avalon's Own** — Morgan le Fay's court. They do not come to mortals. Mortals come to them, or are brought. Meeting one outside Avalon is significant and probably not accidental.

## Blessings and Their Shape

A Fae blessing always has:
- A **condition** (when it applies)
- A **bonus** (+2 to a specific check in that condition)
- A **tell** (something physically or perceptually different about the bearer — a mark, a sound only animals hear, the way water moves near them)

The tell cannot be hidden from other Fae. They know, immediately, that you have been touched.

## Breaking a Bargain

You may break a bargain. The consequences are not immediate. The Fae do not rage. They simply update their accounting. What you owe becomes larger. What you are owed, if anything, may be called in at an unexpected time. The Fae are patient.

A character who has broken three bargains is known among the Sídhe. Doors that were open are now closed. New bargains carry harsher terms.
