# Ravenmoor — The Drowned Village

## Overview
Ravenmoor sits at the edge of the Ashfen Marshes, perpetually draped in grey mist and cold rain. The village was prosperous a century ago, built around the Aldwick silver mines. Then the mines broke through an underground lake and flooded. Forty-three miners drowned. The village never recovered — economically or spiritually.

The dead walk in Ravenmoor. Not dramatically, not all the time — just wrong. A shadow that lingers after the body has moved on. A figure in a window of a house whose owner died last winter. The locals call it "the echo." They've learned to look away.

## Geography
- **The Crossed Road:** The only way in or out. An ancient iron cross marks the junction. Locals leave flowers. They rot quickly.
- **The Aldwick Inn:** The only accommodation. Run by Marta Voss, who charges too much and asks too little.
- **The Sunken Chapel:** St. Aldric's, half-collapsed into the marsh. Still used on Sundays. Father Dorian Vane presides.
- **The Millhouse:** Abandoned. The mill wheel still turns despite no water reaching it.
- **The Aldwick Mine Entrance:** Sealed with iron doors stamped with warning signs in three languages. Sometimes light flickers behind the seal.
- **The Voss Manor:** On the hill above the village. The Voss family has governed Ravenmoor for four generations. Count Aldric Voss III died eight months ago. His son Casimir has not been seen since the funeral.

## Atmosphere
Always raining or threatening rain. Cold even in summer. The trees are thin and black-barked. No birds. Fog rolls in from the marsh at dusk and doesn't leave until noon. The locals speak in short sentences and avoid eye contact with strangers.

## The Secret
The forty-three miners didn't just drown. Something was already down there, in the dark under the underground lake. Something that was waiting. The flood didn't kill it — the flood released it. It's been slowly moving up through the aquifer ever since, following the water table, rising toward Ravenmoor's wells.

## The Deeper Secret
It is not a creature. It is not evil in any morally legible sense. It is old in the way mathematics is old — it existed before there were minds to perceive it, and it will exist after the last mind goes dark. The miners did not disturb it. They provided it with a door. It has been deciding, at a speed no human lifespan can measure, whether to use it.

Father Vane's sealing rite slowed its decision. What happened to Father Vane has accelerated it.

The geometry of the mine tunnels has changed. Not collapsed — changed. The angles are wrong. Surveyors who have gone in do not disagree on what they saw. They disagree on whether what they saw is possible.

## Tone Shift
The campaign has moved past gothic horror into cosmic horror. The threat is no longer the ghost of grief — it is the indifference of something so vast that human suffering is beneath its notice. This does not make it less dangerous. A tide does not mean to drown you.

Write accordingly: less dread of death, more dread of insignificance. Less darkness as menace, more darkness as absence of anything that cares.
